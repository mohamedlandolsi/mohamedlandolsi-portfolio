## The problem

Wavess is a Berlin B2B SaaS startup. Its customers are small B2B companies in Germany, the UK and the Czech Republic that want market signals to turn into LinkedIn content and sales actions.

The gap it works on: market intelligence and content execution live in different tools. Teams carry insights from one to the other by hand, so signals rarely become timely action, and LinkedIn content written by hand is slow to produce and inconsistent in voice.

When I joined for my six-month end-of-studies internship there was one product, the content service, and only its basic post creation worked.

## What I built

Three product services on one shared library, one database and one set of background workers. I did the analysis, the architecture and the implementation, under the CEO's supervision and with code review from the team.

The specification came first: <Metric claim="W3" inline />. I modelled the content and GTM flows as use cases and BPMN processes, and scored the priority of every requirement for the CEO to review. Delivery then ran in <Metric claim="W7" inline />, in three phases.

1. **Rebuild the content service (Tropicc).** It was partly working when I joined, and <Metric claim="W8" inline />: the content feed, organization settings, the generation pipeline and the LinkedIn integration. It writes posts in each client's brand voice, grounds product posts in the client's own documents, predicts engagement before publishing and schedules across timezones. A person approves every draft before it is scheduled.
2. **Extract the shared core and build the Portal.** Authentication, tenant handling and shared utilities had ended up duplicated or scattered. I moved them into one Python library that every service imports, and built the Portal as the one place where people sign up, organizations are created and product access is decided.
3. **Build the GTM intelligence engine (Oceanss).** It collects hiring, media, feedback and financial signals, flags unusual activity against each company's own baseline, scores accounts by hiring intent and gives each client a dashboard built around the goal they chose.

Every request passes <Metric claim="W10" inline /> before it reaches business logic. Every model call goes through one router.

## What broke, and what I changed

**Onboarding trusted the model too early.** The Portal fills a new organization's profile from its website with an LLM, and the flow had to be revised once I saw what extracted profiles looked like in real use. The version that shipped stores the extraction as a draft. The user corrects and confirms it, and only then does it become the organization's record. Running the extraction again later shows a difference per field, and the user picks each change.

**User keywords polluted competitor scans.** The hiring scan builds its search queries from keywords. In competitor scans, keywords typed by users tended to pollute the results, so those scans now build their queries from the model's analysis alone. Customer-profile scans still merge the user's keywords in.

**One feature was cut to ship another.** A competitor generation system for the content service was planned for the last phase. It was deferred so the GTM engine could reach production.

What these numbers do not show: the test suites run against mocked providers, load testing was not built during the internship, and the accuracy of the model-based scoring was not measured.

The router, the quota tracking and the approval step before anything reaches a customer are the patterns I rebuilt on free tools in the [Personal GTM Engine](/work/personal-gtm-engine) and [Job Radar](/work/job-radar).

## What I would do next

- Load tests and tests against live APIs, so behavior with many tenants at once is measured, not assumed.
- Let the two products talk: signals from the GTM engine should suggest topics in the content service. The platform was designed around that link, and it is not built yet.
