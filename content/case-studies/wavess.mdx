## Context

Wavess is a Berlin B2B SaaS startup that helps small B2B companies (about 5 to 100 people) in DACH, the UK and the Czech Republic turn market signals into LinkedIn content and sales actions. During my six-month internship it served <Metric claim="W1" inline />. The team was small and remote, and the work ran in <Metric claim="W7" inline />.

The problem the platform addresses: market intelligence and content execution live in different tools, so signals rarely turn into timely action, and producing LinkedIn content by hand takes hours per profile each week.

## The platform

<Diagram name="wavess-architecture" alt="Three product services, Portal for identity and workspaces, Tropicc for LinkedIn content, and Oceanss for GTM intelligence, all depend on a shared core library that handles token verification, tenant resolution, access gating and LLM routing. - **Portal**: multi-tenant identity, onboarding and workspace governance.
- **Tropicc**: LinkedIn content generation with retrieval over each client's own material and an engagement prediction model.
- **Oceanss**: GTM intelligence that collects hiring and market signals, detects anomalies and scores accounts.
- **Wavess-Core**: the shared library for authentication, tenant context, access control, background jobs and LLM routing.

## Decisions that mattered

{/* Source: the report's trade-off tables (Table 2.15 and 2.17 to 2.22), not decisions.json. Keep each as decision, alternatives, reason. */}

- Federated coarse-grained services over a monolith or fine-grained microservices: independent scaling of content and signal workloads without the operational cost of many services for a small team.
- One database with a schema per service. Tenant isolation through row-level security in the Portal, and mandatory tenant filtering in the content backend (which uses a service key).
- A multi-provider LLM chain with quota tracking over self-hosted models, to stay within a startup budget.

## What I took from it

This is where the GTM Engine and Job Radar come from: the same pattern of configuration first, rules before models, a provider chain, and a human approving anything that reaches a customer, rebuilt on free tools.
